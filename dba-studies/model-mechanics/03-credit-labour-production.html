3. Credit, labour, and production

Randomized matching allocates scarce credit and workers. Production is then capped by whichever input is most restrictive.

InputsCredit requests, bank equity, desired jobs, productive inputs
MechanismAllocate credit → fire/hire → set wages → Leontief output
OutputsLoans, employment, wages, production
1. Allocate credit under two constraints

Borrowing firms are shuffled. Each receives the smallest of its request, its collateral headroom, and the bank’s remaining regulatory lending capacity.

DLᵢ = max(0, min(DLᵈᵢ, ζLTV·Kᵉᵢ−Lᵉᵢ, Eₖ/ζ−ΣLᵉ−ΣDL))

Because allocation is sequential and shuffled, scarce bank capacity can produce different firm-level allocations across runs.

2. Reconcile employment with desired headcount

Vacancies equal desired minus current employment. Randomly ordered employees are fired where vacancies are negative. Unemployed workers and firms with positive vacancies are then repeatedly shuffled and matched.

Vᵢ = Nᵈᵢ − Nᵢ

3. Set effective wage/productivity

The ratio between attainable target output and current labour capacity adjusts the average wage and labour productivity, capped at 1.5.

factorᵢ = min(1.5, min(Qˢᵢ,Kᵢκᵢ,Mᵢβᵢ)/(Nᵢᾱᵢ))wᵢ = w̄ᵢ·factorᵢαᵢ = ᾱᵢ·factorᵢ
4. Produce with Leontief technology

Inputs are strict complements: output is the minimum of the target and labour, capital, or material capacity. Extra capacity in one input cannot replace a shortage in another.

Yᵢ = min(Qˢᵢ, Nᵢαᵢ, Kᵢκᵢ, Mᵢβᵢ)

Finally each employed worker’s wage is copied from their employer.
