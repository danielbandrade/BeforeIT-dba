4. Household budgets

Four household-owner groups estimate disposable income, then reserve fixed shares for consumption and housing investment after tax.

InputsBenefits, wages/profits, taxes, expected inflation
MechanismExpected income → spending propensities → tax adjustment
OutputsC_d_h and I_d_h budgets
1. Index social benefits to expected growth

Both general and inactive-person benefits grow with expected GDP growth before household income is estimated.

sb_other′ = sb_other(1+γₑ)sb_inact′ = sb_inact(1+γₑ)
2. Estimate income by household type

Employed: after-contribution and after-income-tax wages plus the general benefit. Unemployed: a wage-linked replacement benefit plus the general benefit. Inactive: inactive and general benefits. Owners: after-tax dividends from positive expected firm or bank profit plus the general benefit.

Yᵉ employed = [w(1−τSIW−τINC(1−τSIW)) + sb_other]·P_HH·(1+πₑ)Yᵉ owner = θDIV(1−τINC)(1−τFIRM)·max(0,Πᵉ) + sb_other·P_HH·(1+πₑ)
3. Split expected income into budgets

The same rule applies independently to active workers, inactive workers, firm owners, and the bank owner. Dividing by one plus tax converts nominal expenditure into the pre-tax goods budget passed to the market.

Cᵈₕ = ψ·Yᵉₕ/(1+τVAT)Iᵈₕ = ψH·Yᵉₕ/(1+τCF)
4. What happens later?

These are desired budgets, not guaranteed purchases. The goods market may ration them when supply is insufficient. Final income and deposits are calculated only in phase 7 using realised consumption and investment.

ΔDₕ = Yₕ − (1+τVAT)Cₕ − (1+τCF)Iₕ + deposit interest − borrowing interest
