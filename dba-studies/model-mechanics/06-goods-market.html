6. Goods market

For each product, buyers are randomly matched with domestic firms and import supply. Lower-priced and larger sellers are more likely to be selected.

InputsDesired purchases, production, inventories, imports, prices
MechanismFirm-input market → retail market → aggregate results
OutputsRealised purchases, sales, sector price indices
1. Build seller-selection weights

Domestic and foreign sellers enter a common pool for each good. Selection weight adds a preference for low prices to a preference for large available supply.

weight(f) = exp(−2P_f)/Σexp(−2P) + S_f/ΣS
2. Firms buy materials and capital goods

Each firm’s demand for a product combines its technology-weighted material demand and product-weighted investment. Buyers and sellers are repeatedly sampled until demand or supply is exhausted.

demand(i,g) = a[g,Gᵢ]·DMᵈᵢ + bCF,g·Iᵈᵢ

If current goods run out, a second pass can use firms’ remaining production capacity. Spending is separated afterward into realised materials and investment.

3. Households and institutions buy retail goods

Household consumption and housing investment, export demand, and government demand are allocated by product. A purchase is full when the selected seller has enough stock; otherwise the buyer takes the remaining stock and continues searching.

retail demand(h,g) = bHH,g·Cᵈₕ + bCFH,g·Iᵈₕ
4. Aggregate and write back realised trades

The model sums product-level trades into household consumption/investment, firm input purchases, government consumption, exports, and seller demand. Quantity sold cannot exceed supply.

Qᵢ = min(Yᵢ + Sᵢ, Qᵈᵢ)Qₘ = min(Yₘ, Qᵈₘ)

Household capital increases by realised investment. When parallel=true, product markets run concurrently and a lock protects shared household totals.
