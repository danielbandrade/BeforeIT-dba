1. General estimations

The model turns past observations and correlated random innovations into expectations, external conditions, and interest rates used by every later phase.

InputsGDP/inflation history, euro-area state, covariance matrix
MechanismForecast → shocks → Taylor rule → bank spread
OutputsY_e, gamma_e, pi_e, rates
1. Forecast domestic GDP and inflation

estimate_next_value fits the historical series through the current period. Expected GDP is the exponentiated log forecast; growth compares it with latest GDP. Inflation is also transformed back from log space. Open the forecasting deep dive →

Yₑ = exp(forecast(log(Y)))γₑ = Yₑ / Yₜ₋₁ − 1πₑ = exp(forecast(π)) − 1

2. Draw correlated external innovations

A standard-normal vector is multiplied by the Cholesky factor of prop.C. This preserves the calibrated covariance between euro-area GDP, exports, and imports.

[εY_EA, εE, εI] = cholesky(C) × N(0, I)
3. Evolve the euro-area economy

Euro-area GDP and inflation follow log autoregressive processes. Growth is the new GDP divided by its previous value.

Y_EA′ = exp(αY·log(Y_EA) + βY + εY)γ_EA = Y_EA′/Y_EA − 1π_EA′ = exp(απ·log(1+π_EA) + βπ + επ) − 1
4. Set policy and commercial-bank rates

The central bank applies a non-negative Taylor rule with rate smoothing. An optional shock runs immediately afterward. The bank then adds the calibrated risk premium.

r̄′ = max(0, ρr̄ + (1−ρ)[r* + π* + ξπ(π_EA−π*) + ξγγ_EA])r = r̄′ + μ

Order matters: a rate shock changes cb.r_bar before the commercial bank calculates bank.r.
